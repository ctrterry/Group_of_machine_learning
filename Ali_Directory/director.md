# Hyperparameter Selection for ANN

In our Artificial Neural Network architecture, hyperparameters were carefully selected to optimize the model's predictive power for director impact on movie ratings. Our analysis tested various combinations of batch sizes (32, 64), hidden layer node counts (32, 64, 128), learning rate (0.01), activation function (ReLU), momentum (0.9), and regularization parameter (0.001). After systematic testing, a batch size of 64 with 32 hidden nodes produced the best performance (R² = 0.4356), outperforming more complex architectures. The relatively small hidden layer size suggests that the relationship between director attributes and movie ratings can be effectively modeled without excessive complexity, while the larger batch size (64) provided better gradient updates during training. ReLU activation was preferred for its ability to mitigate vanishing gradient problems, and a modest learning rate of 0.01 offered stable convergence. The momentum value of 0.9 helped navigate the loss landscape efficiently, while the small regularization parameter (0.001) prevented overfitting without excessively constraining weight magnitudes.

# K-fold Cross-validation Explanation

K-fold cross-validation (with 5 folds) was utilized in our experimental design to ensure robust model performance evaluation. This technique divides the dataset into five equal segments, using four segments for training and the remaining segment for validation in each iteration, repeating this process five times with different validation segments. This approach offers several advantages for our director impact analysis: it maximizes the use of our limited dataset by allowing each data point to serve in both training and validation roles; it reduces the risk of misleading performance estimates that might result from a single arbitrary train-test split; and it provides a more reliable assessment of how well our model generalizes to unseen data by averaging performance across multiple data partitions. With our final model achieving a consistent R² value of approximately 0.435 across folds, we have confidence that the director score's explanatory power for movie ratings (explaining roughly 43.5% of rating variance) is reliable and not merely an artifact of a particular data division.
